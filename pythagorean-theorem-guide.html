The Pythagorean Theorem

In any right triangle, the square on the longest side equals the sum of the squares on the other two. It is one of the oldest and most useful results in mathematics.

a² + b² = c²

Definition 1.1

The parts of a right triangle

A right triangle has one angle of exactly 90 degrees. The side opposite that angle is the hypotenuse, always the longest side, usually called c. The two shorter sides, called the legs, are a and b.

Statement 1.2

What the theorem says

a² + b² = c²

If you draw a square on each side, the areas of the two smaller squares add up exactly to the area of the largest. It works only for right triangles, and it works for every one of them.

Example 1.3

A worked example

A ladder leans against a wall. Its foot is 6 feet from the wall and it reaches 8 feet up. How long is the ladder?

  • a = 6, b = 8
  • 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100
  • c = √100 = 10 feet
Remark 1.4

Why it is true

Place four copies of the triangle inside a big square of side a + b so their hypotenuses form a tilted square in the middle. The big square’s area can be counted two ways: as (a + b)², or as four triangles plus c². Setting them equal and simplifying gives a² + b² = c².

Summary 1.5

Where you will use it

Finding distances on a map or a coordinate grid, checking that a corner is square in carpentry, working out screen sizes and much of trigonometry all rest on this one idea. Triples like 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13 are worth remembering.
