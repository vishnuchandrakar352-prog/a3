How to Read a Proof

A proof is a chain of small, certain steps that leads from what we accept to what we want to show. Learning to read one slowly is the best way to learn to write one.

P ⇒ Q

Definition 2.1

What a proof is

A proof is an argument that shows a statement must be true, every time, without exception. It starts from definitions and axioms (things agreed at the start) and earlier theorems, and uses logic to reach a conclusion.

Statement 2.2

The common shapes

  • Direct proof: assume P, reason step by step, arrive at Q.
  • Contradiction: assume the opposite, and show it leads to something impossible.
  • Induction: show it is true for 1, and that truth for n gives truth for n + 1.
Example 2.3

A tiny example

Claim: the sum of two even numbers is even.

2m + 2n = 2(m + n)

Any even number can be written as 2 times a whole number. Adding two of them gives 2 times another whole number, which is even. ∎

Remark 2.4

How to read slowly

Read each line and ask: why is this allowed? Which earlier fact does it use? Write the step in your own words. If you get stuck, try the argument with real numbers. Drawing a picture often helps.

Summary 2.5

Symbols you will meet

⇒ means “implies”. ⇔ means “if and only if”. ∀ means “for all” and ∃ means “there exists”. ∴ means “therefore”, and a small square ∎ marks the end of a proof.
